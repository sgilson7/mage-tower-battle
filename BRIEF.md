## The tool

A 3D browser game for up to four players. Each player is a new archmage at a mage college whose library the fired previous archmage wrecked on his way out. A match is three to five days. Each day has a few minutes of preparation and then a battle:

- **Preparation** (the player acts). The archmage walks around one shared room in the tower: picks up books from the library floor to learn spell pieces, combines pieces into spells at the spell laboratory, recruits students at the recruitment table through an interview mini-game, teaches spells to students at the podium by drawing the spell's symbols with the mouse, edits the defence layout around the tower, and reads the invasion book that shows what the coming battle will bring. Walking between stations costs time, so planning the order of tasks is the skill.
- **Battle** (the player watches). Every player's tower is attacked at once, tower-defence style, in a 3D physics simulation with ragdolls. Students cast what they were taught from the tower's offshoots and sub-towers, at the effectiveness the teaching earned. Invasion spells add enemies to rivals' battles. Players watch all towers side by side.

The device is a desktop or laptop browser with a keyboard and mouse.

## Done means

To be agreed with the person in `PLAN.md` before any code. Proposed:

- one full match of 4 days can be played from the first day to the result, alone against three computer-played rivals, and online with up to four players;
- each tower's score only goes down, by the damage the tower takes, and the highest score after the last day wins;
- a spell built from a payload, a modifier and a trigger casts as its parts say, in the core tests and on screen;
- teaching accuracy changes a student's effectiveness by the rule in the glossary, and the same drawing scores the same on every machine;
- an invasion cast by the archmage appears in the rival's invasion book at once, and one cast by students appears only when the battle starts;
- no immunity: every damage type harms every enemy, at the half, normal or double rate the type chart gives;
- `./scripts/check.sh` passes in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit.
